# Motivation

I love the vibe and rawness of custom built ergo keyboards. But for my first serious custom build I wanted to achieve a more commercial look and finish. Since I had no CAD experience, it was also an excellent chance to learn hardware design, electronics and million of other skills I never thought I'd need to know.

# Design

I had clear design and functionality requirements: wireless, split, ortholinear, low-profile, aluminum case.

- <mark>Wireless</mark> — is essential, especially with splits. Placing both sides anywhere without dealing with TRRS
  cables is a no-brainer. Removing my right side to make space for mouse movements still feels magical.
- <mark>Ortholinear</mark> — is what I'm used to, my two previous keyboards were Nyquist and Helix. And I've gotten
  pretty proficient with them at around 70-90wpm.
- <mark>Sans stagger</mark> — I'm not against row or col stagger, I just love the look of a neat pain grid. And easier
  to work with for hardware beginners. I'll revisit this in the next iteration.
- <mark>Layout</mark> — was a deliberate choice to avoid context switching as I often swap between my Macbook and
  desktop. I also went with 17x17mm switch spacing to keep the product small.
- <mark>Aluminum</mark> — for aesthetics and to get that finished commercial look. Sacrificing RF signal, potential ESD
  issues I was cool with.
